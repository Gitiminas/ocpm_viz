# Decisions

Settled decisions are binding — implement as given. Items marked 🎓 are worth a supervisor
sanity-check but **do not block**. Open questions are listed in §2 and must be resolved before the code that depends on them is written.

Everything here is thesis material: the rationale columns are the raw form of the design chapter.

| 22  | **The sequence spine is drawn per object in the issue and PR lanes**, and per lane only in the commit lane.                                                                                                                                                                                            | With one issue and one PR the two are identical, which is why the mockup could draw it per lane. | 23  | **Sub-lanes are allocated by greedy interval partitioning over an object's *visual* extent** — its intervals *and* its event times — and touching extents may share a band.                                                                                                                             | Greedy partitioning uses the minimum number of bands, so a band appears only when overlap genuinely forces one (decision 3). Touching extents share a band because the span does not overlap; the collision loop separates the two glyphs at that instant. |
| 24  | **A link is coloured by the object it reaches, not by its qualifier**, and reaches that colour at 45% of its length rather than at the very end. Each object in a lane gets its own shade within the type's hue (`objectShade`), applied to its glyphs, its lifecycle bar and the far end of every link arriving at it. Shade index 0 is exactly the type's base colour. | Hue identifies the *type*; with two PRs in a lane nothing identified the *individual*, so a link arriving in the PR lane could not be traced to a PR. The far end alone is useless for this — that is precisely where links converge and overlap — hence the 45% weighting. ⚠️ Supersedes qualifier-as-colour: for `related_issue` and `referenced_pull_request` the qualifier colour already equalled the target's type colour, so nothing is lost; only `referenced_issue` changes, and it had been borrowing the *evidence* palette, conflating a relation with its certainty when certainty is already carried by the dash. Visible on F1: that one link and its "?" badge turn from orange-red to the issue's blurple. |
| 25  | **Links that would be drawn on top of one another are fanned apart** by a few px, and only those.                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Not cosmetic. A commit inside two overlapping PRs' spans yields two straight vertical links at the identical x, and the one reaching the nearer band is a strict sub-segment of the other — completely hidden. In F5 the buried link was the *extracted* one, so the dashed line and its "?" badge, the S8 uncertainty cue the tool exists to show, vanished under a solid line. Restricting the fan to links that actually coincide leaves already-diverging links exactly where they were. |
| 26  | **Commits are toggled in groups by the PR they reach, never individually**, and a commit is visible while **any** group it belongs to is visible (union).                                                                                                                                              | A real sub-log holds hundreds of commits, so a per-commit list is unusable; "the commits of PR #x" is the unit people reason about. Hiding "PR #1's commits" must not strip a commit out of PR #2, which is still on screen and to which the commit genuinely belongs. | 27  | **Anything hidden is always declared.** `summarizeHidden` reports every subtraction, the banner is non-dismissible while anything is hidden, and drawing the timeline and refreshing the banner are the *same function* so the two cannot diverge. Lane captions read "1 of 2 objects" when filtered.     | The cost of presets and filtering is hidden dependencies (Green): a sparse timeline reads as "this project had few links" when the truth is "you switched the links off", and the picture alone cannot distinguish those. In a task-based evaluation that misreading would go straight into a finding. Every control that can subtract therefore reports, and each item restores individually so undoing one subtraction does not discard the rest of a configuration. |
| 28  | **A preset captures what is *shown*, not where you were looking** — zoom, scroll, focus and burst expansion are excluded — and applying one **merges** rather than replaces.                                                                                                                            | Restoring someone else's scroll position is noise, and focus is a transient probe rather than a configuration. Merging means an old preset, or one shared from a different sub-log, applies what it can instead of blanking toggles it has never heard of; a stale object id simply hides nothing. Presets are also URL-encodable, which is the reproducibility mechanism for the task-based evaluation: "load this link" is the cheapest guarantee two people on two machines see the same thing. |
| 29  | **Statistics are computed over the *visible* sub-log, and folding is not filtering.** `selectVisible` yields the objects, events and links currently on screen; a collapsed burst still contributes all of its commits.                                                                                 | Decision 12 requires metrics to respond to the toggles, which only means anything if they are computed over what is actually shown. Demonstrated on F1: hiding comments moves the longest idle gap from 184h to **112h** — and *downwards*, because F1's longest gap runs to a comment eight days after the merge, so hiding comments deletes the tail rather than merging two gaps. A precomputed figure would have answered "unchanged" to a question whose real answer is neither obvious nor monotonic. Folding is excluded because collapsing a push is a statement about density, not about scope; conflating them would make the commit count jump from 32 to 13 when a burst is folded. |
| 30  | **Aggregates report median and range, never a mean**, and objects with no finished interval are excluded from the duration rather than counted as zero.                                                                                                                                                 | Process durations are strongly right-skewed: one issue open for a year drags a mean to a value that describes neither the typical case nor the outlier. The range is what tells the reader whether the median means anything. Counting an unfinished object as zero would understate every aggregate it appears in, silently.                                                                                                                                          |

| 49 | **Both endpoints of a link are objects.** `findGraphProblems` rejects a link whose `source` or `target` names an event, naming which end and saying that it is an event. Taken while implementing 41: the ten `qualifier === 'actor'` guards are deleted rather than kept as defence, and F1's builder proves the dropped links redundant — a bijection with `event.actor_id`, checked in **both** directions — before it will write the fixture. | The validator accepted an event id *because* the actor links existed; with them gone the leniency only hides a mistake.
